# Provenance

**Read this before writing a line of narration.** Which route applies
decides what the video is allowed to claim, and rewriting narration after a
render means regenerating every audio file.

A testimonial makes an assertion about a real person's experience. Three
routes produce an honest video; anything else produces a fabricated one.

## The three routes

| Route | What it is | Requires | Disclosure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **quoted** | The customer said this. TTS reads their words. | Written permission on file, and a record of where the quote came from | Yes — the voice is recreated |
| **recorded** | The customer recorded their own voice. | Written permission on file | No — the voice is real |
| **illustrative** | Nobody said this. A composed example. | Nothing | Yes, and prominently |

## What each route may claim

**quoted** — only what the person actually said, in their words. Tightening
for length is fine. Adding a number they did not give, or sharpening a
vague statement into a specific one, is not: it puts words in a real
person's mouth.

**recorded** — whatever they recorded. This is the strongest route and the
only one with no synthetic element.

**illustrative** — anything that is *plausible and not attributed*. Invent
the persona, the company and the numbers if you like, but the video must
never suggest a specific real person said it. The moment an illustrative
testimonial is presented as a real one, it stops being illustrative.

## Disclosure

The disclosure goes **burned into the frame**, not in a video description
that travels separately from the file. Defaults per route:

| Route | Text |
|---|---|
| `quoted` | `Cita literal de {atribución}. Voz recreada.` |
| `recorded` | *(none — the voice is real)* |
| `illustrative` | `Testimonio ilustrativo. Persona y datos ficticios.` |

**For `illustrative`, put it on the opening card as well as the closing
one.** A clip trimmed to its first fifteen seconds for a feed loses the end
card, and with it the only thing separating an example from a claim. Two
cards cost nothing and the video stays disclosed however it is cut.

In the opening card the disclosure belongs in `subtitle`; `title` carries
the attribution under the metric.

## What this skill will not build

A video of a person — generated or otherwise — presenting themselves as a
customer and asserting results that did not happen. Labelling the project a
prototype does not change what the file is: it works as a fake testimonial
the moment it leaves the machine, and fabricated endorsements are treated as
deceptive advertising in most jurisdictions, with specific rules for
reviews and testimonials.

Every legitimate goal behind that request is reachable through the three
routes above. `illustrative` in particular allows a complete, polished,
publishable video with invented people — it just has to say so on screen.
